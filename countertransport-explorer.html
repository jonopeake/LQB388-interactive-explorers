Countertransport

Watch how the sodium–hydrogen exchanger couples sodium entry to hydrogen ion exit through the same membrane protein.

Na⁺ · SodiumH⁺ · Hydrogen ion

What supplies the energy?

Sodium moves in down its electrochemical gradient. This drives hydrogen ions out against their electrochemical gradient under the conditions shown.

The exchanger uses the energy of sodium entry. The sodium–potassium pump uses ATP to help maintain the sodium gradient. The exchange is an example of secondary active transport.

Why does it matter?

Removing hydrogen ions can help a cell recover when its internal pH falls.

Compare with symport

Countertransport: Sodium enters and hydrogen ions leave. The two substances move in opposite directions.

Symport: Sodium and glucose both enter through the sodium–glucose cotransporter. The two substances move in the same direction.
