Will this input trigger an action potential?

Here, an input is a signal from another neuron. It releases neurotransmitter at a synapse on the dendrite, opening ligand-gated channels and causing a local depolarisation. Different neurons can form synapses at different points along the dendrite. Use the distance slider to choose which synapse is active.

Depolarisation and hyperpolarisation

Both examples start at −70 mV. Compare the local graded potentials produced when a neurotransmitter changes which channels are open.

Depolarisation

Binding of an excitatory neurotransmitter such as glutamate to its receptor can open cation channels. At −70 mV, more positive charge enters as sodium than leaves as potassium. The membrane potential becomes less negative, producing an excitatory graded potential in this example.

Hyperpolarisation

Binding of an inhibitory neurotransmitter such as GABA to certain receptors can indirectly open potassium channels. At −70 mV, potassium moves out overall, carrying positive charge with it. The membrane potential becomes more negative, producing an inhibitory graded potential in this example.
